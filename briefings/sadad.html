AURA × SADAD
Bill presentment and payment connection

SADAD is Saudi Arabia’s national electronic bill presentment and payment system. It can support AURA journeys where an employer, service provider or approved intermediary is entitled to issue a bill. The attached gateway guide defines bill creation only; AURA’s biller/sub-biller status and the notification, refund and settlement interfaces are not confirmed.

01 · Executive summary

SADAD is a payment rail; biller access and end-to-end reconciliation decide the value

Official system

National invoice payment through bank channels

SAMA describes SADAD as a national bill presentment and payment system connecting invoice issuers to banking and non-banking channels, including licensed digital wallets. It supports biller and intermediary models.

Project guide

One proposed gateway operation

The 28 September project guide defines a POST bill-creation route through an ePayment intermediary, with a large integer bill number, decimal amount, expiry string and returned bill number. The route is not itself proof of biller status or SADAD ownership.

End-to-end gap

Payment confirmation is separate

SAMA describes automatic payment notifications and reconciled/settled reports at the SADAD system level. The attached guide does not define those API routes, how AURA receives them, refunds, or the bank settlement contract.

Board conclusionKeep SADAD in Wave D—Discovery. Reassess the baseline from 44 to 46/100 because SAMA publicly documents biller/intermediary services and notification/report capabilities. Access remains unverified for AURA, and the update is not enough to move into build.
02 · Purpose and customer use cases

Use SADAD when an authorized biller needs an invoice reference paid through banking channels

The commercial case is a biller or sub-biller collection journey, not a general card checkout. AURA can create the payable in its workflow, show the SADAD bill reference, and track payment after an approved status interface is secured.

Employee Hub value

AURA can show why a bill exists, who issued it, amount, expiry, reference and payment status. The employee pays through their chosen bank channel and can return to the service. This can reduce unclear payment steps and manual receipt chasing.

Direct confirmation in AURA requires the authorized biller/intermediary notification or query route; bill creation alone cannot complete the journey.

Value to measure

  • Payment completion and time from bill creation to payment.
  • Expired or duplicate bill references and support contacts.
  • Unmatched payments, settlement delays and reconciliation exceptions.
  • Manual invoice creation and payment follow-up effort.
  • Employee confidence in the amount, issuer and payment outcome.

First end-to-end flow

  1. Employee chooses a permitted paid service and sees who is charging and why.
  2. AURA confirms the order and validates amount/expiry under customer rules.
  3. AURA creates the bill through the approved intermediary and stores returned bill number.
  4. Employee pays using a participating bank or licensed wallet channel.
  5. AURA receives an approved payment event, marks the case paid and later reconciles settlement.

Reliability and security

  • Use TLS verification, secret/network controls, timeouts and local mock tests.
  • Use BigDecimal for monetary values; never derive payment amounts with floating-point arithmetic.
  • Track pending, paid, expired, rejected, refunded and settlement states separately.
  • Do not blindly retry bill creation after an ambiguous timeout; reconcile first to avoid duplicate bills.
  • Keep bill/payment data out of routine logs and restrict refunds to finance roles.
06 · Operating model and controls

Make every bill traceable from approved service to settlement

Payment operations

  • Persist AURA payable ID, provider bill number, biller, amount, expiry, creation result and timestamps.
  • Link payment notification, bank transaction reference, refund and settlement references to the same payable.
  • Display “paid” only after a verified provider status—not from user screenshot or bill creation response.
  • Handle late, duplicate, invalid, expired and reversed payment notices with an exception queue.
  • Reconcile transaction totals to settlement reports before closing a period.

Financial and employee controls

  • Use customer-approved payables and finance approval rules; keep AURA out of the role of biller unless contractually appointed.
  • Show payer the biller identity, purpose, amount and expiry before bill issuance.
  • Protect employee and transaction identifiers; use role-based access and tenant isolation.
  • Set refund limits, maker-checker approval and immutable audit history.
  • Do not treat a rejected or delayed status as a reason to retry payment without checking original bill state.
Payment boundarySADAD provides bill-presentment/payment through bank channels. PayTabs-style card checkout, merchant acquiring, card-token handling and hosted-payment links are different products and should not be represented as SADAD capabilities.
