01 · What is it?

A specific agent-registration status interface

Regulatory context

Umrah companies and agents

The Ministry of Hajj and Umrah licenses Saudi Umrah service companies and qualifies external agents. Official rules describe an MOI security-clearance role connected to the licensing workflow. [1] [2]

Gateway contract

MOI create / update

The supplied guide defines two bearer-token operations: POST /DoCreateOmrahAgent and POST /DoUpdateOmrahAgent. [4]

Aura scope

Controlled adapter

A backend service that submits an already-approved agent record, validates the nested business response and stores an auditable outcome.

Scope boundaryThe contract’s NICRequest class name refers to Umrah-agent create/update. It is not person lookup. The guide does not establish access to Absher, visas, permits, pilgrim records or broad MOI services.
02 · Value for Aura

Reduce manual handoff for one regulated back-office process

Best fit: a licensed Umrah operator or authorized platform already responsible for maintaining approved external-agent records. This is an operational partner-management connector, not a broad employee self-service feature.

Less re-keying and a consistent audit trail between approved agent onboarding and the government gateway.

Limited reach

Only organizations participating in this regulated Umrah-agent workflow would use it.

Critical dependency

The integration owner must define who may create/update records and how unknown outcomes are reconciled.

06 · Access, prerequisites & blockers

Not build-ready without an approved owner contract

Confirm before build

Prerequisites

  • Exact service owner and the roles of MOI, Ministry of Hajj and Umrah and any gateway operator.
  • Eligible customer/operator, purpose, legal basis and production entitlement.
  • Approved sandbox/production URLs, token issuance/rotation and network/certificate rules.
  • Agent ID, permit number and status-code catalogs with required/optional fields.
  • Accepted and rejected examples for both create and update.
  • Read/reconciliation process, rate limits, SLA and incident contacts.
Delivery controls

Guardrails

  • Allow calls only after internal approval and role checks.
  • Keep tokens server-side and redact identifiers from logs.
  • Validate IDs, permit and status against owner-approved catalogs.
  • Do not auto-retry writes or convert missing responses to success.
  • Use synthetic sandbox data and minimize retained agent information.
  • Audit actor, purpose, before/after values, correlation and result.

07 · Cost

No verified public API price

The public licensing inquiry is free for end users, but that is not the price or permission for a third-party write integration. Gateway commercial terms, onboarding, support and the customer’s regulated operating model require written confirmation.

Provider access

Onboarding, environments, token issuance, network controls, service fees and SLA.

Compliance

Legal ownership, privacy/security review, audit design and regulated process validation.

Aura delivery

Discovery, adapter, approval workflow, durable state, reconciliation, tests, monitoring and support.

Total = provider onboarding/fees + Aura implementation + governance/security assurance + recurring monitoring and support
Quote requiredAsk the confirmed integration owner for pricing and terms against the exact customer, operations, volumes, environments, data fields, SLA and support model. Do not use public Umrah-company inquiry fees as an API estimate.

Board decision

Fund service-owner discovery only. Proceed to a synthetic sandbox proof after the owner, authority, two operation contracts, status catalog and reconciliation path are approved. Do not market a broad “MOI connector.”
