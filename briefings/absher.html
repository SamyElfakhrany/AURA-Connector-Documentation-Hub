02 · Product and access scope

What Absher is—and what the evidence does not show

Official service platform

Absher describes itself as the Ministry of Interior’s electronic platform serving citizens, residents, and visitors. The official landing page links to three portals:

  • Absher Individuals: e-services for citizens, residents, and visitors.
  • Absher Business: e-services for establishments and business owners.
  • Absher Government: e-services for government-sector employees.

Published access evidence

  • Public pages direct people to use Absher’s online/app service channels and sign in.
  • Absher Business documentation describes a company/establishment owner authorizing another registered user to perform a specified service through that user’s Absher Business account.
  • The official sign-in page offers “Login via Nafath” and username/password login for the human user.
  • These are portal and user workflows; they do not by themselves document a third-party AURA API.

Direct connector design requirements if approved

  • Use the exact auth and API protocol specified by the provider. Do not infer OAuth, API-key, or Absher account credential flows from the internal guide.
  • Keep credentials server-side, scoped to the approved customer/tenant and operation.
  • Minimize national ID/Iqama data, log no credentials or full sensitive payloads, and require purpose/role checks before calls.
  • For write operations, add durable request state, provider reference, duplicate handling, and unknown-outcome reconciliation.
  • Represent user-initiated handoffs separately from verified provider status.
  • Preserve a manual/service-desk fallback for provider outage or an ineligible user.
  • Do not automate browser sign-in, scrape authenticated pages, or simulate OTP/Nafath approvals.
  • Record provider acceptance and terms before reusing a route across AURA apps or customer tenants.
